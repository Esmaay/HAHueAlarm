HAHueAlarm · design brief & reference teardown

Wake the room,
not just the sleeper.

A bedside alarm for iOS/Android built in Expo. At alarm time it does two things at once: it ramps your Philips Hue lights from dark to daylight through Home Assistant, and it plays the alarm sound. A sunrise you can’t sleep through.

Dark-first UI Realistic sunrise ramp Home Assistant REST 15 apps studied on Mobbin
01The concept

One alarm, two outputs — light and sound, fired together.

Standard alarms only own the speaker. HAHueAlarm also owns the room. When an alarm fires the app calls a Home Assistant service to run a gradual Hue sun-up while the local sound plays — and both stop when you dismiss.

T‑minus

Ramp begins early

Optional pre-alarm: Hue fades from a 1% ember up to bright, so you surface from light sleep before the sound.

Alarm time

Light + sound fire

App plays the tone and sends the Hue brightness & colour to Home Assistant over its REST API.

Wake gate

Prove you’re up

Optional shake-to-dismiss so you can’t swipe the alarm off half-asleep.

Dismiss

Room stays lit

Sound stops; lights hold at full daylight (or run a “morning” scene) so you actually get out of bed.

02Design language

Warm charcoal by night, daylight-amber at the moment of waking.

The interface lives on a nightstand in a dark room, so the ground is a warm near-black — a neutral with a deliberate amber bias, no cold blue. A single accent, the Hue “daylight” glow, turns up only where waking happens: the sunrise ramp, the active toggle, the fire moment. Everything else stays quiet.

Create / edit an alarm

Reference: Me+ · Eight Sleep · Amazon Alexa · Apple Clock
  • Wheel picker up top, settings list below. The universal alarm layout — time anchored at the top, tappable rows for everything else. Don’t reinvent it.
  • Text “Cancel / Save”, not an icon. This is the iOS-native pattern (Apple Clock, Alexa, Sonos). A checkmark icon reads as ambiguous when you’re half-awake — words state the outcome. A bottom “Save alarm” button (Hatch) is the main alternative; we go native.
  • “Rings in 12h 24m.” Eight Sleep & Me+ show time-until live under the picker. Cheap to build, reassuring to see.
  • Our first-class rows: Hue lights and Sunrise sit right beside Sound — the integration is a headline feature, not buried in settings.

The ringing screen

Reference: TIDE · Pillow · Endel · Opal
  • Full-bleed, single focus. Huge time, one line of context, two actions. TIDE & Endel keep it almost empty — nothing to fumble half-awake.
  • Snooze is big and forgiving; Stop is deliberate. Pillow uses “press & hold to stop” so you don’t kill it by accident — we mirror that.
  • The screen reflects the room. The background warms in real time as the Hue lights ramp — UI and physical light rise together.
  • Must survive a locked phone. Fires as a full-screen notification / foreground service, not just an in-app view.

The wake mission (anti-oversleep)

Reference: Pillow (shake) · Me+ (Alarmy-style) · Rise · WHOOP
  • The category’s killer feature. Alarmy built a business on “complete a task to silence it.” We ship shake-to-dismiss first: shake N times, tracked by the accelerometer.
  • Physical & hard to fake. Shaking forces movement and both hands — far better at breaking sleep inertia than a swipe.
  • Pairs perfectly with Hue. While you shake, the room keeps brightening — light + motion together beat sound alone.
  • Adjustable intensity, and optional. Set the shake count, or turn the mission off for a plain hold-to-stop. Math/QR missions are a fast-follow.

Sunrise & smart wake

Reference: Hatch Sleep · Rise · Bevel · Apple Health Bedtime
  • This is our differentiator, so it gets its own screen. A real sun-up curve: brightness climbs 1%→100% while colour temperature warms from a 1800K ember through amber to a 4500K daylight finish — mapped straight onto Hue’s brightness_pct + color_temp_kelvin.
  • Ramp-before-alarm. Hatch & Bevel start the light in a window before wake time to catch light sleep. Duration presets: 10 / 20 / 30 / 45 min.
  • Light styles. Warm wake (gentle, tops out amber), Natural (true sunrise to daylight), Energize (fast, bright, cool-white finish).
  • Post-dismiss handoff. Hold daylight, run a Home Assistant scene, or turn off.

04Distilled best practices

The non-negotiables the top apps share.

Reliability is the product

An alarm that misses once is uninstalled. It must fire when the app is killed, the phone is locked, and offline — via OS scheduled / full-screen notifications, never a JS timer.

Gradual > jarring

Every sleep leader (Hatch, Apple, Rise) ramps light and volume up. Sudden max-volume waking is last-gen. Our Hue sun-up is this principle made physical.

Degrade gracefully

If Home Assistant is unreachable, the sound alarm still fires — the light is an enhancement, never a single point of failure. Show a quiet “couldn’t reach Hue” note after.

Half-asleep ergonomics

Big targets, forgiving snooze, deliberate stop (hold / shake). Assume blurry eyes and clumsy thumbs in the dark.

True dark, low glare

Warm desaturated ground, dimmed disabled states, no pure-white flashes. The screen shares a nightstand with a sleeping person.

Setup that isn’t scary

Connect to Home Assistant once, then a friendly picker lists your real Hue lights & scenes. A clear “test connection” success state removes the fear.

Cross-platform, honestly: one codebase, two alarm engines.

Expo ships both iOS and Android from a single codebase and the dark UI is identical on both — only small platform manners differ (Android back gesture, top-bar action, edge-to-edge). The real divergence is how each OS lets an alarm fire, and it runs counter to intuition: Android is the more capable alarm platform.

Android · permissive

AlarmManager.setAlarmClock() fires exact through Doze; a full-screen intent launches the ringing screen over the lock screen; a foreground service loops audio. Native alarm-app behaviour — we get the full experience.

iOS · constrained

Notification-based: bundled sound (~30s cap), Critical Alerts entitlement to break through silent/Focus. A true full-screen takeover from a killed state isn’t guaranteed — so the Hue light, fired by HA on schedule, is a real reliability backstop.

Permissions we’ll request. Android — POST_NOTIFICATIONS, SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM, USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT, plus a friendly “disable battery optimisation” prompt for rock-solid firing. iOS — notifications and (optionally) the Critical Alerts entitlement. Both handled with clear, in-context asks — never a wall of dialogs on first launch.

The Hue bridge, concretely. The app talks to Home Assistant’s REST API. A sunrise is a sequence of light.turn_on calls (or one call with a long transition) targeting the chosen entities, stepping brightness_pct and color_temp_kelvin from warm-dim ember to bright daylight. Post-dismiss can call scene.turn_on. Every network call is wrapped so a Hue failure never blocks the sound alarm.

Phase 1 · Foundation

Scaffold + dark design system

Expo Router project, theme tokens from section 02, the Lucide icon set, navigation, and the alarm list & create/edit screens with local persistence. A working, beautiful alarm shell — no integrations yet.

Phase 2 · It actually rings

Reliable alarms + ringing screen

Scheduled notifications, looping audio with volume ramp, full-screen dismiss UI, snooze, hold-to-stop. The make-or-break reliability work.

Phase 3 · The Hue moment

Home Assistant integration + sunrise

HA connect & test screen, entity picker, the sunrise ramp engine (brightness + colour temperature), and firing light + sound together. Graceful degradation baked in.

Phase 4 · Delight

Shake mission & polish

Shake-to-dismiss, onboarding, sound library, settings, micro-interactions, and the live-warming ringing background.
